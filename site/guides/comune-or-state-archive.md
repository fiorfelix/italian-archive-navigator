# Italian civil records: Comune or State Archive?

Every Italian civil record was written twice. Knowing where each copy went tells you whom to ask, and where the extra papers are.

## Two originals, signed together

Italian civil registration began on 1 January 1866 under the Royal Decree of 15 November 1865, n. 2602; areas that joined Italy later started when they joined. Each birth, marriage and death act was written in **two originals**, signed at the same time by the parties, the witnesses and the registrar. At the end of each year:

- one original stayed in the **Comune's archive**;
- the other was deposited at the **registry of the local court** (*cancelleria del tribunale*).

State offices transfer to the State Archives the records of matters closed for more than thirty years. So for older years the court's original is usually now at the **State Archive** (*Archivio di Stato*) of the province. When each court series was transferred differs between archives, and some are still being transferred.

## The papers that exist only once

The supporting documents filed with marriage acts (*allegati* or *processetti*, such as the spouses' birth records and parents' consent) and the register of marriage banns were bound with the **court's** series only. If you need them, ask the State Archive (or the court, for recent years), not the Comune.

## Which one to ask

- **Recent records:** the Comune. It also decides what it can release about recent events and living people.
- **Older records:** either. The State Archive's copy is often the easier one to consult, and many State Archive registers have been photographed and published on [Portale Antenati](https://antenati.cultura.gov.it/), the Ministry of Culture's portal. If one original is lost or damaged, ask for the other.
- **Marriage allegati and banns:** the State Archive.

## Before 1866

It depends on the state the place belonged to. Many areas had Napoleonic civil registration in the early 1800s, and some pre-unification states kept civil registers after it; others, such as the Papal State in Lazio and Umbria, had no civil registration after the Restoration, so parish registers (baptisms, marriages, burials) are the main source. Parish registers are kept by the parish or the diocesan archive.
