When your ancestor's Italian town changed its name

The place name on an old document is often not the name of any comune today. Here is why, and how to find the office that now holds the records.

Why the name may not match

  • Renamed comuni. Many comuni changed their names after 1861.
  • Merged or split comuni. Small comuni were joined to others, and some were later restored.
  • Hamlets. Many villages were never comuni: they are frazioni or localities inside a larger comune, which keeps their records.
  • New provinces. Provinces were created and redrawn, so the province on an old record may not be the province today.
  • Same name, different places. The same name can belong to several localities in different parts of Italy.

An example: Corneto

According to ISTAT's record of administrative changes:

  • 1871: Corneto, in the province of Rome, became part of Italy;
  • 1872: it was renamed Corneto Tarquinia;
  • 1922: it was renamed Tarquinia;
  • 1928: it moved to the new province of Viterbo.

So a birth "in Corneto, province of Rome" in 1880 is today in the comune of Tarquinia (VT), and that is the Comune to write to. There are also small hamlets called Corneto in other regions, which is why the province and the year matter.

How to find the right comune

  1. Note the place name exactly as written, the year, and the province if given.
  2. Look it up in ISTAT's official lists: the codes of today's comuni, the history of changes since 1861, and the census list of localities and hamlets.
  3. Write to today's comune, or to the State Archive of today's province for older records. Mention the old name in your letter.
