How to request an Italian ancestor's birth record

Italian birth, marriage and death records can be requested by letter or certified e-mail. Here is who to write to, what to put in the request, and which kind of copy to ask for.

1. Find the office that holds the record

For Italian civil registration (from 1866 in most of Italy), the record exists in two originals: one at the civil registry office (Ufficio di Stato Civile) of the Comune, and one that went to the court and, for older years, is usually now at the State Archive (Archivio di Stato) of the province. Recent records are requested from the Comune. See Comune or State Archive? for how to choose.

Write to today's comune. If the town has been renamed or merged since your ancestor's time, find out first which comune it belongs to now: when the town changed its name.

2. Choose the kind of copy

The civil registry issues three kinds of document from its records:

  • Certificate (certificato): states the person's details and the references of the act.
  • Extract (estratto per riassunto): a summary of the act and its annotations.
  • Full copy (estratto per copia integrale): an exact transcription of the act with every annotation, certified as matching the original. The law allows it only when someone with an interest in the act asks for it explicitly, and only where the law does not forbid it.

For family history a full copy says the most (parents, witnesses, later annotations), so say clearly that you want a copia integrale and why you have an interest in it, for example that the person is your direct ancestor.

3. Write the request

  • The person's full name and, if known, the parents' names.
  • The event (birth, marriage or death), its date or year and the place.
  • The act number and year, if you found them in an index.
  • The kind of copy you need, and your interest in it.
  • Your name, postal address and e-mail, and a copy of an identity document.
  • A request to be told any fees and how to pay them.

Italian public offices publish their certified e-mail (PEC) in IndicePA, the official index of public administrations; the law requires them to set up at least one PEC mailbox and list it there. Many offices accept requests sent to it. Fees, accepted channels and response times differ between offices, so ask rather than assume.

Records of living people, and recent records, are protected: the Comune decides what it can release and to whom.
