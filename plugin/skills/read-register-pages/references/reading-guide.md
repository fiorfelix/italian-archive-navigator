# Reading guide: Italian registers, 19th–20th century

Typical wording and conventions, to help reading. These describe common practice, not rules:
registers vary by place and period, so trust the page in front of you.

## Civil registers (stato civile), from 1866

- **Opening:** "L'anno milleottocentottanta, addì cinque di marzo, a ore dieci antimeridiane,
  nella Casa comunale. Avanti di me … Sindaco / Assessore delegato, Ufficiale dello Stato Civile
  del Comune di …, è comparso …". The date of the act is the date of the declaration, not
  always of the event: the event's date and hour usually follow ("è nato … il giorno …").
- **Numbers and dates in words:** "milleottocentottanta" = 1880; "millenovecentodue" = 1902;
  "addì" = on the day; "ore antimeridiane / pomeridiane" = a.m. / p.m.
- **Persons:** "figlio di … e di …"; "fu" before a name = deceased ("di fu Giuseppe" = son of
  the late Giuseppe); "domiciliato / residente in …"; "possidente" (landowner), "contadino"
  (farmer), "bracciante" (day labourer), "casalinga" (housewife), "calzolaio" (shoemaker).
- **Births:** informant (often the father or the midwife, "levatrice"), the child presented,
  given names imposed, two witnesses. Foundlings: "esposto/a", a given surname.
- **Marriages:** spouses' ages, places of birth and residence, parents; banns (pubblicazioni)
  dates; witnesses. The supporting papers (allegati) are in a separate volume.
- **Deaths:** age, place of death, spouse ("vedova/o di …", "marito/moglie di …"), informants.
- **Margins:** act number and name; later annotations of marriage, death, recognition,
  rectification, citizenship.

## Earlier civil registers

- **Napoleonic** (about 1806–1815): printed forms in Italian or French; similar structure;
  ten-year tables may exist.
- **Restoration states** (to 1865): varied by state; often printed forms, sometimes completed
  from parish records.

## Parish books

- Often in **Latin**: "Ego infrascriptus Parochus baptizavi infantem … natum ex … coniugibus",
  "patrini" (godparents), "in matrimonium coniunxi", "obiit", "sepultus est".
- **Latinised names:** Ioannes (Giovanni), Iosephus (Giuseppe), Franciscus, Dominicus, Antonius,
  Petrus, Maria, Anna, Catharina, Angela; genitive endings ("filius Ioannis" = son of Giovanni).
- **Months:** "7bris" = September, "8bris" = October, "9bris" = November, "Xbris" or "10bris" =
  December.

## Military records

- **Liste di leva:** by comune and birth class; name, parents, date and place of birth,
  outcome of the call-up.
- **Ruoli / fogli matricolari:** by district and class; description (height, hair, eyes),
  occupation, literacy, service history, campaigns, discharge; later annotations.

## Handwriting traps

- Letters often confused: u / n, c / e, rn / m, long s / f, a / o, i without a dot / e.
- Doubled consonants are often written once, and vice versa; "j" for "i" (Bagnaja = Bagnaia).
- Abbreviations with a raised final letter or a stroke: "Gio." (Giovanni), "Gius.e"
  (Giuseppe), "Franc.o" (Francesco), "Ant.o" (Antonio), "q.m" or "fu" (the late).
